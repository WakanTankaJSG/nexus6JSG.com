01
The Construct

Inverting the problem

The classical Turing test asks whether a machine can produce convincing human behaviour. The Retro Turing asks something different and deeper: does a machine have a sufficiently rich internal model of humanity to recognise it when encountered?

Classical Turing
Production

Can the machine generate behaviour that a human cannot distinguish from another human? Requires only statistical imitation.

Retro Turing
Recognition

Does the machine have a generative model of humanity? Recognising is not imitating — it requires understanding what makes behaviour genuinely human.

What it measures
Internal model

Whether artificial systems have generative models of humanity or only statistically learned decision boundaries. A test of inverse artificial theory of mind.

Temporal scale
Multi-layer

Humanity inscribed at three scales: milliseconds (biological kinematics), 100ms–s (interactive temporal organisation), minutes (dynamic coupling between interlocutors).

03
The judge system

Two-layer architecture

Layer 1 is a general-purpose multimodal model (GPT-4o / Gemini) that classifies and produces structured output. Layer 2 is 7 parallel analytic modules that do not classify — they extract features for post-hoc analysis.

This architecture allows us to know not only whether the judge is correct, but which signals were available and whether the reported reasoning correlates with the features that actually drove the decision.

04
Theoretical framework

Beyond the Ekmanian paradigm

The framework begins from the empirical refutation of basic emotions theory and rebuilds from a functionalist, ecological, and constructionist base.

Fridlund (BECV)
Displays as social tools
Facial expressions are not readouts of internal states. They are signals calibrated to the interactive context to influence the interlocutor. Foundation of M1 and M7.
Fernández-Dols
Expression / emotion dissociation
Olympic gold medallists smile 76% during interactive periods, 3% during non-interactive ones — with identical self-reported emotional state. Expression is a function of social context.
Crivelli · Russell
Minimal universality
The fear face is interpreted as threat among Trobrianders. Only valence and arousal cross cultures reliably. Discrete categories are cultural constructions.
Lindquist
Psychological constructionism
Emotion = core affect + conceptual knowledge. AI has the cultural script but not the core affect. Prediction: its expressions will be statistically more prototypical.
Yik · Russell
Valence-arousal circumplex
The only dimensional metric with robust cross-cultural validity. Affective behaviour characterisation uses circumplex coordinates, not discrete categories.
Eibl-Eibesfeldt · Birdwhistell
Biological vs. cultural
Biologically grounded signals (eyebrow flash ~100ms, articulatory anticipation) are harder to replicate than cultural ones (kinesic stress). Directly testable hypothesis.
05
Predictions

Five falsifiable hypotheses

01
Scalar dissociation
AI systems fail differently at each temporal scale. Errors in layer 1 (milliseconds) are more frequent and systematic than in layer 2, which are more frequent than in layer 3.
02
Social vs. semantic calibration
AI systems produce displays contingent on semantic content but not on social context. The interactive/non-interactive display ratio will be flatter in AI than in humans (Fernández-Dols paradigm).
03
Excessive prototypicality
AI expressions will be statistically more prototypical: lower intra-individual variance, greater fit to the cultural archetype. The script without the core affect produces humanity that is too perfect.
04
Biological › cultural in synthesis difficulty
Biologically grounded markers — eyebrow flash timing, articulatory anticipation — will be harder to replicate than culturally grounded ones. Eibl-Eibesfeldt vs. Birdwhistell operationalised.
05
Unreliable judge introspection
The channel the judge reports as dominant will not predict its errors as well as the layer 3 modules. Evidence of limited meta-representation in artificial systems about their own decision process.
